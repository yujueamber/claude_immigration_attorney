# NIW Narrative Review Guidelines

> Practical drafting and evidence-organization rules for EB-2 NIW review.
>
> These are **not USCIS regulations** and are **not a substitute for Matter of Dhanasar, the USCIS Policy Manual, current USCIS guidance, or attorney review**.

## Core Principle

Employer projects are **evidence**, not automatically the proposed endeavor.

A strong NIW narrative should move from:

```text
Employer-specific contribution
        ↓
Recurring cross-organization technical problem
        ↓
Specific technical gap
        ↓
Reusable/generalizable method
        ↓
Implementation plan
        ↓
Verified outcomes
        ↓
Credible broader implications
```

Do not use this logic:

```text
Major employer is important
→ employer project is important
→ proposed endeavor is nationally important
```

Use this logic instead:

```text
Production work demonstrates capability
→ applicant generalizes and advances a concrete technical method
→ the endeavor has a credible path to impact beyond one employer
```

## Prong 1 — Specific Endeavor, Not Generic Field Importance

Do not rely mainly on:
- AI market size
- generic STEM importance
- employer reputation
- product customer count
- general growth in speech AI

Prong 1 must answer:

### 1. What specific problem is being addressed?
- What technical gap exists?
- Is the problem broader than one employer?
- Is there independent evidence that the problem exists?

### 2. What exactly is the proposed method?
- What components does it contain?
- What is reusable/generalizable?
- How is it different from ordinary job duties?
- What will be built, tested, validated, or disseminated?

### 3. What are the broader implications?
- What is the mechanism of impact?
- Can the method reduce duplicated engineering?
- Can it improve interoperability, security, reliability, deployability, or auditability?
- How can organizations other than the current employer use or benefit from it?

Potential applicability alone is not enough. Explain **how impact could occur**.

## Prong 2 — Well Positioned

For every anchor accomplishment, use:

```text
Background
→ Applicant's specific role
→ Technical method/decision
→ Verified outcome
→ Evidence
→ Connection to proposed endeavor
```

Rules:
- Every major accomplishment must connect to the proposed endeavor.
- Separate applicant contribution from team contribution.
- Separate completed results from planned results.
- Do not invent quantitative outcomes.
- Use release records, PRs, design docs, tests, public docs, manager/project-lead letters, and independent assessments where appropriate.

## Recommendation Letters

Avoid praise-only letters.

A useful letter should establish:
- the problem,
- applicant's specific contribution,
- why it was technically meaningful,
- the outcome,
- how the recommender knows,
- why it supports the proposed endeavor.

Independent experts may evaluate industry need, technical method, or broader relevance, but should not attest to internal facts they did not observe.

## Prong 3 — Independent Balancing Analysis

Do not copy Prong 1 into Prong 3.

Prong 3 must answer:

> Why, on balance, would waiving the job-offer and labor-certification requirements benefit the United States in this applicant's actual circumstances?

Do not fabricate:
- inability to obtain a job offer,
- inability to obtain PERM,
- employer sponsorship barriers,
- a requirement that the applicant work independently of employers.

## Mandatory Self-Tests

### Employer Removal Test

> If the employer and product names were removed, would the proposed endeavor still be clear, specific, technically meaningful, and capable of broader impact?

If no, the endeavor is too employer-dependent.

### Generic Engineer Test

> Could another engineer in the same field use the same proposed endeavor with only superficial edits?

If yes, the endeavor is too generic.

## Evidence Classification

Every important statement must be tagged mentally as:

- **Established Fact** — directly supported by evidence.
- **Supported Analysis** — reasonable inference from evidence.
- **Future Plan** — genuine activity the applicant intends to pursue.
- **Unsupported/Speculative** — remove, narrow, or obtain evidence.

## Claim-to-Evidence Rule

For every major claim ask:
1. What exactly am I claiming?
2. What evidence proves it?
3. Does it prove the applicant's contribution or only a team result?
4. Does it prove current impact or only potential impact?
5. Which Dhanasar prong does it support?
6. Am I asking the evidence to prove more than it actually proves?

## Fast Reviewer Test

The first page should quickly answer:

1. **What?** What exactly will the applicant do?
2. **Why?** Why does the specific problem matter beyond one employer?
3. **Why this applicant?** What completed work proves execution ability?
4. **Why waive?** Why does the balancing favor a waiver?

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Do not equate employer importance with national importance.
2. Do not treat an employer project as the proposed endeavor.
3. Do not use generic AI importance as the core Prong 1 argument.
4. Do not use vague innovation language when a concrete mechanism can be stated.
5. Do not attribute team results to the applicant without evidence.
6. Do not present planned GA, open source, adoption, or impact as achieved.
7. Do not treat open source, downloads, or broad applicability as automatic proof of national importance.
8. Do not let recommendation letters become praise-only documents.
9. Do not copy Prong 1 into Prong 3.
10. Do not invent evidence, metrics, novelty, adoption, or immigration constraints.
11. Every anchor accomplishment must connect to the proposed endeavor.
12. Every anchor accomplishment should have a real outcome or an explicit evidence gap.
13. Every broader-impact claim must identify a mechanism of impact.
14. Separate fact, analysis, plan, and speculation.
15. Make the core case understandable on the first page.
